## The author

I'm Leslie Johnson, a product designer in Pittsburgh. My work has moved from interfaces to systems, and I'm most interested in how people work alongside autonomous ones.

In a university research lab, I designed software you could talk to. For a longitudinal health study, I designed a voice-enabled tablet from scratch that read each question aloud, so participants with low reading fluency could answer on their own. Then I watched field teams in rural Appalachia run the study, and redesigned their onboarding. At a payments company, I saw how students struggled to pay in distracting environments, and redesigned the flow to be as simple as autopay.

Now the machines move. A car hands the wheel back at highway speed; a drone holds position until someone says go. The design of those moments is what this pattern library is about.

I started this work as a deep dive into autonomous systems, and as a way to name and sort the patterns I've uncovered. Patterns stay drafts until I find clear evidence, and the open questions on each page are ones I haven't answered yet. If you work in autonomy, robotics or human factors and something here is wrong or missing, [please tell me](mailto:leslie@lesliejohnson.io).
